3. XIRR (item 8)

Is the formula right, are the inputs right, and does the screen say what the number means?

What the user sees today

On the Mutual Funds Overview, the summary strip has an XIRR tile with a switch between Lifetime (“Since inception”) and Current (“Current holdings”). The tooltip says: “Annualised return since inception. May include an opening balance from before you started tracking in Unifolio.” Each fund row in the holdings table also has its own XIRR. The number is shown to one decimal place, and as “—” when it cannot be computed.

XIRR tile

Made-up numbers. The middle state is what finding F1 does: one fund with no price is dropped from the final amount but its purchases stay, so the rate falls from 9.4% to 6.1% with nothing on screen to say why. After the fix the fund is valued (FX-8a), so the tile reads 9.4% again. There is no warning note: the problem is removed, not announced.

How it is calculated (read in code)

  1. The solver (backend/app/services/analytics/xirr.py): finds the rate that makes the discounted cash flows add up to zero, with each flow discounted by (actual days since the first flow) / 365. That is the same convention as Excel’s XIRR. It uses exact Decimal arithmetic (no floats), starts from 10%, and falls back to a bracketed search when it does not converge. It returns nothing when there is no money in and money out.
  2. The flows (dashboard/xirr.py): purchases, SIP instalments, opening balances and gifts received count as money put in (negative, with stamp duty); redemptions, dividend payouts, reversals and gifts given count as money taken out (positive); the current value is added as a final inflow dated today. Reinvested dividends, bonus units and segregated units are not cash flows.
  3. Switches: the Lifetime figure leaves them out (they are internal moves). The Current-holdings figure and the per-fund figure count a switch-in as money put in and a switch-out as money taken out.
  4. Lifetime vs Current: Lifetime uses every transaction, including funds you sold. Current uses only the funds you still hold.
  5. Opening balances: a statement that starts mid-history carries an “opening balance” dated at the statement start, not its real purchase date. If a figure is above 35% and removing that undated piece would bring it back under 35%, the tile shows a disclaimer with an alternative figure.

4. Rank denominators (item 10)

The “7” in “7 out of 33” is a rank among funds. The “33” is how many funds were counted, and that is what stakeholders found different from Advisorkhoj. Our job: find out why, fix what is ours, and bring the number to the best accuracy we can.

How the investigation runs (your framing)

You are confident our method is right and suspect the denominator goes wrong somewhere, which also throws off the rank. That is a sound suspicion and a useful one to test, because the rank is 1 plus the number of pool funds with a higher return: a pool that is wrong changes both numbers together. So the order is:

0. Terms firstCheck Advisorkhoj’s terms of use before reading anything (task A-6).

2. Is ours right?Rebuild our pool independently and compare step by step. Include the end-date mix (F7).
3. Where does ours go wrong?If anywhere: which step, which funds, how much it moves the total and the rank.
4. ProposalsFix proposals for what is ours; evidence for what is theirs; a choice for what is only a definition.

I will not assume either site is right at the start. Your confidence in our method is a hypothesis we test in step 2, and if it holds, the evidence says so.

What the user sees today

On the Performance tab, each held fund has a rank for 1Y, 3Y and 5Y. Each is “rank of total”, and the total is different for each period.

Made-up fund and numbers. Notice 33, 29 and 21: the count shrinks as the period gets longer.

How the number is made (read in code)

  1. The universe. The AMFI daily price file (NAVAll.txt), every scheme in the same SEBI category. Category spellings are merged first (the same function Batch C uses for 17a).
  2. Live funds only. A scheme whose last price is more than 30 days old is dropped (closed funds and matured plans).
  3. One series per fund. Schemes are grouped by fund house and base name. Of each group, the series with the same plan type as the held fund (Direct with Direct, Regular with Regular) is kept, preferring the Growth option. A held IDCW plan is ranked by its fund’s Growth series, and the screen says “ranked as”.
  4. Excluded from the pool: inactive schemes, and any index fund or ETF (by name words or category).
  5. A return for each period. Price on or before the start date and on or before today; 1Y, 3Y and 5Y return from those two prices. A fund with no price on or before the start date has no return for that period and is left out of that period’s count only.
  6. The rank is 1 + the number of pool funds with a strictly higher return (equal returns share a rank). The total is the number of funds in that period’s pool.

5. Category average comparison: calculation and the Excel (item 9)

Prove the numbers are right, fix anything that is not, and build the detailed Excel for stakeholders. This work moved here from Batch E. The legend and the “i” icon wording stay in Batch E, and they use this batch’s result.

What the user sees today (read in code)

Made-up fund; the three rows use your three examples. A filled dot is the fund, a hollow dot is the category average; green means ahead, red means behind (from the card’s own legend line). The sentence under the rows says how many funds the average covers for each period.

How the average is calculated (read in code)

  1. Pool: the same pool as the rank: same category and plan, one Growth series per fund, live in the last 30 days.
  2. Eligibility floor: a fund must have a 3-year return to count in any period. So the 1Y average is over funds that also have 3 years of history.
  3. Weighting: each fund’s return is weighted by its latest average assets under management (AAUM). A fund with no size on record is left out of the weighted figure and counted. If no fund has a size, a plain average is shown and flagged.
  4. Three periods (1Y, 3Y, 5Y), each with the number of funds it covers. A category with fewer than 5 funds is flagged “thin”.
  5. A legacy figure (40% of the 3Y and 60% of the 5Y return, weighted the same way) is still sent for desktop, print and phone views “until the cleanup release”.
