Item 3, 4 and 5 rules (answers of 31 Oct)

The rules behind the market cap note, the main-manager choice, the LinkedIn check, and what the overlap page does when a category has no funds or one fund.

Item 3: what is "6% of your equity could not be sorted", and why show it?

The market cap card does not decide a company's size. It looks every stock up in AMFI's half-yearly list, which puts the top 100 listed companies in Large, the next 150 in Mid and the rest in Small. A stock is left out of all three when the list has no row for it:

  • Overseas stocks (an international or Nasdaq fund) are not Indian listings, so AMFI never classes them.
  • Stocks added after the list's date (a recent listing) are not on it yet.
  • Delisted, merged or renamed stocks, and rows where the fund house gives no ISIN, cannot be matched.

You asked for Large, Mid and Small to add to 100%. We do that by dividing by the part we could sort. The note says what the 100% is of. Without it, a family with a large international fund would read "Large 54%, Mid 31%, Small 15%" as a split of all their equity, when it describes 94% of it. The note costs one line and is shown only when the unsorted part is 0.5% or more; it can sit in the info tooltip instead. It is optional in the sense that the chart works without it, but it is what keeps the three numbers honest. This is D8, answered with option A.

Item 4: which manager gets the fund's value

Yes: the main manager gets the whole fund value (D7 option A), so a co-managed fund is no longer split. Manager totals can therefore add to at most the portfolio value, and each fund counts once. The factsheet roles actually seen in our parsers are: Assistant Fund Manager, Overseas Investments, Equity Portion, Debt Portion, Gold/Silver Instruments, Dedicated Fund Manager for ..., Equity / Debt / Commodity / Equity/Arbitrage (LIC's layout), and no role at all (most houses). LinkedIn (ON HOLD 31 Oct): no link until the profile is verified, and how we know it is the right person

No link is shown, and none is stored as usable, until a person has verified it. We never show a search page and never guess. A profile is accepted only when these checks are written down:

  1. Name. The profile's name matches the factsheet name (allowing an honorific, a middle initial or a different order).
  2. Employer in the headline or current position. The headline or current role names the same fund house, by its brand or legal name (for example "Fund Manager at Nippon Life India Asset Management", "Portfolio Manager, HDFC AMC"). This is the strongest check, as you suggested.
  3. Role. The title is an investment role (fund manager, portfolio manager, head of equities, chief investment officer, analyst), not an unrelated job.
  4. Dates. The experience entry at that fund house covers the "managing since" date printed in the factsheet.
  5. Second source, when available. The fund house's own page for the manager (name, photo or qualifications) agrees with the profile.

Rule: at least checks 1, 2 and 3 must pass, and one more of 4 or 5. If two profiles both fit, or the profile is private or shows no employer, or the person has left the fund house, we do not link.

Process: a person finds the candidate and writes down what they saw (the headline text, which checks passed, the URL, the date); a second person approves it before it can show (two-person check). Nothing is matched or scraped by code. The link is stored once per manager and fund house in fund_managers with columns for name key, fund house, URL, evidence note, checks passed, verified on, verified by, reviewed by and re-check due. The card shows the icon only when a verified, unexpired row exists.

Staying right: the link expires and is re-checked every 6 months, and straight away when a monthly import shows the manager at a different fund house or off the fund. A failed re-check hides the link automatically. The link opens the profile in a new tab with rel="noopener noreferrer".

Item 13: the Others slice

  • Five slices: Equity, Debt (including cash), Gold, Real Estate (REITs and InvITs) and Others.
  • Others holds everything not placed in the four: positive derivative lines and rows the fund house leaves unclassified or labels "other". It is grey, always last, and hidden when it is 0%.
  • Negative derivative lines (futures that hedge an arbitrage fund, for example) are still ignored and counted in the coverage note, so Others can never be negative.
  • Clicking Others lists the funds behind it, like any other slice.

Design rules for every changed screen

These apply across the app, not only to the item they came up in. Each one names the files that must change.

1. Fund logos, everywhere a fund or fund house is named

The shared component is SchemeLogo. The Mutual Funds list and the Individual Fund page already use it. It tries the fund house's Google favicon, then Clearbit, then the logo links passed to it, and falls back to a tile with the first letter. The rule: wherever a fund name or fund house name is shown, show its logo the same way, in size small for lists and large for page headers. Fund house rows (By AMC, manager's fund house) use the fund house logo.

  • Already done: Mutual Funds list, Individual Fund page header, Category ranking and Fund ranking rows, the TER ladder, the fund comparison header.
  • To add on screens in this plan: Valuation by Fund (new), Performance Ranking and Category Average Comparison (kept), the Fund Manager card (fund house and fund rows), By AMC card and its drill-down, Asset Class and Market Cap drill-downs, all Overlap parts (heatmap headers now show initials chips, top pairs, pair detail, common holdings, stat strip, impact), Holdings All Stocks fund names, Performance fund rows, Benchmark rows, Transactions ledger and period drill, SIP card, Home Movers and its pop-up, Individual Fund overlap panels, Quick Insights.
  • Later, lower priority: the legacy dashboard, planning and scenario rows, the phone app's cards, the print view.
  • Two cautions. Logos are fetched at view time from Google and Clearbit, so a blocked network shows the fallback tile, and each page view makes outside requests; a local copy of the 35 logos would remove both problems. The fund-house matching uses a loose substring rule on names, so a test should check every logo against the real list of fund houses.

2. One heading per card

On Other Analytics each group has a heading (from GROUP_TITLES) and its card has its own heading. Renaming both to the same words prints the title twice, which is what happened in the first mockup of item 8. Rule: the group heading is the name; the card's heading must say something different or be removed. Performance Ranking: group "Performance Ranking", card "Rank in your fund's category". Category Average Comparison: group "Category Average Comparison", card "Returns against the category average". The titles are repeated in tests and docs, so they change together.

3. Plain words in banners

Every top banner states what the number means in one short sentence, then gives the answer, and avoids jargon (no "pct", "TRI", "XIRR" without a word of explanation). The two new banners: Performance Ranking, "A rank such as 4/38 means this fund is 4th best out of 38 funds in the same category. Lower is better." Category Average Comparison, "The number is your fund's return minus the average return of funds in the same category. Plus is better."

4. Green buttons

One brand green, #22C55E, with white text, through the shared button. See item 1 for the contrast figure. A cleanup moves the ~257 hard-coded copies to the token.

5. Real numbers before building

The mockups use made-up numbers. Before each run, the analytics recompute is run on a real household so the golden tests and the final screenshots use actual values. Commands are in the playbook.
