2. What kind of video

Your decision from the list: pure AI-generated, or stylised motion graphics over a real screen capture. There is one more option worth knowing about.

ApproachWhat it isStrengthWeakness
A. Realistic: a real CAMS capture with light animation (my pick; Look 2 in section 3)A short real screen capture of the CAMS form (with dummy details), cleaned up and wrapped in Unifolio’s look: highlights, zooms, callouts, a friendly cursor, branded intro and outro.Shows what users will really see, so nobody is lost when the real form appears; creative and branded without inventing anything; easy to re-cut a single scene when CAMS changes.Needs one clean capture of the real form; the CAMS form appears on screen.
B. Stylised: fully illustrated, no real CAMS pixels (Look 1 in section 3)The whole video is animated artwork, including a redrawn, simplified version of the CAMS form.Most unique and fully ours; no third-party screen shown.If the redrawn form differs from the real one, users hesitate at the real form; more design work.
C. Fully AI-generated videoScenes generated from prompts, including the form and the inbox.Fast and eye-catching.AI tools cannot reliably draw a specific real website; text on screens comes out wrong or garbled; a tutorial that shows the wrong thing is worse than none.
My recommendation: A, with AI used where it is safe. Use AI tools for what does not have to be exact (an intro animation, background, a friendly illustrated transition, music, a voice-over if you choose one) and the real capture for the parts a user must recognise (the CAMS form and the inbox). This keeps the creativity you want without the risk of showing a screen that does not exist.
Another route worth knowing: no video file. The same story could be an animated step-by-step walkthrough built in code inside the pop-up (the same cards, highlights and cursor moves, drawn live). It never goes stale in the way a recording does, works in any language by changing text, and adds no file to host. It is less cinematic and cannot show the real CAMS site. It is offered as option C in Q3.

4. The link and the pop-up

A quiet link under the screen’s heading, a pop-up that plays the video, and the same link on the waiting screen.

Screen

Made-up details; laptop view. The video is drawn as a still.

PartWhat it does
The link“Watch how it works (1 min 20 s)” with a small play icon, under the heading line on the Request from CAMS screen. It is plainly secondary to the green button. The same link also appears on the waiting screen, near “Already got the email?”, because that is where users get stuck on the later steps. Nothing downloads until the link is pressed.
The pop-upThe app’s existing dialog, wide enough for a 16:9 video. Title “How to request your statement from CAMS”, the video, a close button, and below it the four steps as chips that jump to that moment (chapters). The page behind stays where it is; pressing Escape or outside closes it.
ControlsPlay and pause, a seek bar, captions on by default (so it works with the sound off), mute, and full screen. All usable from the keyboard.
When it endsThe pop-up offers the green “Request Statement on CAMS” action and “Watch again”. Pressing the action closes the pop-up and does exactly what the main button does today.
LoadingThe video file is fetched only when the pop-up opens; a still picture shows first. The pop-up never auto-plays with sound.
Hosting (default)A single video file (about 80 s at 720p, a few MB) kept with the front end’s own files or in our own storage, with a captions file. This avoids a third-party player that would set its own cookies on a screen where the user is about to hand over a financial statement. If the file turns out to be too large for the front end bundle, it moves to our storage and the pop-up points to it.
Reduced motionIf the user’s device asks for less motion, the video does not start by itself and the pop-up shows the steps as text beside it.

5. Accuracy and privacy rules

The video is a promise about someone else’s website, so it follows rules that keep it truthful and keep personal data out of it.

RuleWhat we do
Show only what existsNo invented screens. Parts a user must recognise (the CAMS form, the inbox) come from a real capture. Generated or illustrated scenes are limited to the opening, transitions and the ending.
Same words as the appThe video uses the live CAMS form’s own words (F7), because that is what users will see. Where the app’s screen text differs (“10-year duration”), I propose the screen text be corrected, for your review; the two must agree before release.
No personal dataThe capture is made with a dummy email address, a placeholder password and no PAN or real statement. Nothing from a real account is shown. The video is reviewed frame by frame for stray details (browser tabs, bookmarks, autofill, notifications).
Third-party siteCAMS’s name and page appear because the tutorial is about using it. No claim of partnership is made; the narration says “the official CAMS website” because the app’s own text says that. I would ask your manager to confirm this is acceptable before publishing; this is not a legal opinion.
When CAMS changesThe video is cut in scenes, so only the scene that changed is re-made. The screen’s text steps stay the source of truth. A short check of the live form is added to the staging checklist for any release that touches the import screens.
No tracking beyond oursNo third-party player. The only measurement is Batch G’s optional labelled events (opened, started, finished), with no user details.
AccessibilityCaptions on by default, a text version of the four steps beside the player, keyboard controls, and no auto-play with sound.
