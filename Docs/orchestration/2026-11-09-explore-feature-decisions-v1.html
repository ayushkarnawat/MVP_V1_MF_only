Explore Feature Decisions

Planning only. Nothing is built or changed. This page answers one question first: of everything Unifolio computes, what can run for a fund the user does not hold, with no portfolio and no cash flows? It then lays out what it would take, what could go wrong, and the decisions only you can make.

Drafted 9 Nov 2026 on leapswitch-redesign. Sources: the codebase (a read-only audit plus four spot checks), the analytics PDF gap analysis, the Individual Fund spec, the nav shell contract, and the data resources reference.

How to use this page. Sections 1 to 10 are findings. Section 11 holds the questions, each with options and my pick. Choose what you like, add notes, and press Copy my answers. Paste the result into the chat and I will turn it into the design.

1. Verdict

Explore is feasible, and most of the hard maths already exists. The work is in scale, search and coverage, not in new formulas.

3 of 15

2 of 15
are portfolio-level but have a browse twin (AMC, category)
2 of 15
are user-only: XIRR and investment/withdrawal
0
search, list or screener endpoints exist today
  • One fund at a time already works. Every /fund-page/{scheme_id}/... route except /summary serves a fund nobody holds. A test asserts this (test_not_held_scope_still_serves_fund_level_routes). Only the header route returns 404 for an unheld fund.
  • Many funds at once does not work yet. Scores, peer stats and ranks are computed on the request and held in process memory. Nothing stores them per fund for the whole market. A list of 50 funds with scores would trigger category-wide computation 50 times.
  • Prices are fresh only where users already are. The nightly price job refreshes held funds and their category peers. Every other category depends on a one-off backfill, so a non-held fund can show stale numbers with no warning.
  • Coverage is uneven. Doc-level counts suggest managers for about 62% of schemes, expense ratios for about 49%, holdings for about 56%. See section 7 for what these numbers can and cannot tell us.
  • Two non-technical questions come first: whether ranked fund lists count as investment advice for us, and whether Explore is for signed-in users only. Both are in section 11.

2. What Explore is, and how it sits with what exists

Today every number in Unifolio starts from the user's own funds. Explore reverses the starting point: it starts from the market, and the user's portfolio becomes an optional lens.

TodayExplore
Entry point is a held fund (Home, lists, Mutual Funds page).Entry point is search, a category, a fund house or a filter.
The Individual Fund page is "held funds only" (decision L6). A fund nobody in the household holds is a 404 on /summary.The same page, in a "not held" mode: no personal tiles, all fund-level sections on.
Insights come from the user's portfolio (overlap, XIRR, allocation).Insights come from the fund versus its category and benchmark. Overlap becomes "overlap with what you hold", an optional lens.
Search is on hold (L46). Another session is now planning page-wise search.Explore needs fund search ("All funds" group of decision L39). That is a shared dependency with the search session.
Planning has a card named "Explore market scenarios".The word Explore is already used in the product. See question Q6.
Your intent, as I read it. Show people funds they do not own, explain how those funds work, and give basic analytics on them. Not the user's portfolio, and not funds they already hold. I have taken "not already invested in" to mean a filter we can apply (hide held funds), which does use the user's holdings in a trivial way. Q5 asks you to confirm.

6. What stops it scaling from one fund to a catalogue

The maths is not the problem. The problem is that every result is computed when someone asks, for one fund, and the data underneath is kept fresh only for funds users hold.

The blockers, in order of how much they matter

#BlockerWhat happens todayWhat it needs
B1Price freshness outside held categoriesThe 06:00 job warms held funds plus their category peers (checked in refresh_nav_daily.py). Other categories rely on a one-off backfill. Reads do not check freshness, so a stale fund looks normal.A daily price job for the whole Explore universe. AMFI's daily file already carries every scheme's NAV, but per the audit it is not stored in nav_history. To confirm in the spec phase.
B2No per-fund stored metricsScore, peer series and ranks are computed per request and cached in process for 15 minutes to 6 hours, per worker. fund_scores is written but nothing reads it.A nightly job and a table with one row per fund. B4Header route returns 404 for an unheld fundsummary.py raises 404 when nobody in the household holds the fund (checked).A not-held branch. The response already allows empty tiles and has a held flag.
B5Writes during GET requestsScoring commits a row, the category universe can insert schemes, and price warming can download full history inside a request.Move these into jobs before exposing the catalogue to many users.
B6Plan type and peer rulesPeers are one series per fund, same plan type, Growth preferred. Funds not in the latest AMFI file within 30 days are not peers.A rule for how Direct and Regular siblings appear in a list (Q3).

8. Three ways to build it

These are about how, once you have settled what. I recommend the third.

A. Open the door, compute on demand

Add search and a not-held mode, reuse every fund-page route as it is. No new table, no new job.

Good: smallest build. Honours the earlier "no new table" stance. Search plus fund page is a real feature.

Bad: no lists, no screener, no "top funds". First open of a category costs 2 to 4.5 s per fund and repeats per worker. Stale prices go unnoticed.

Fits: single fund discovery only

B. Precomputed Explore layer

New nightly job and metrics table. Lists, categories, screener and compare read from it. The fund page can read from it too.

Good: fast lists, honest freshness stamp, no writes on GET, one source of truth for ranks.

Bad: a new table and migration, a heavier nightly job, and the whole universe now needs fresh prices.

Fits: category pages, screener, similar funds

C. Phased: A, then B my pick

Phase 1: search plus the fund page for any fund. Phase 2: price job and metrics table, then category pages. Phase 3: screener, compare, similar funds, "what if".

Good: something real ships early. Phase 2 is justified by measured numbers from Phase 1, which is the condition the earlier spec set for a cache table.

Bad: two rounds of design. Phase 1 will show stale or empty funds until Phase 2 lands, so Phase 1 needs a visible "as of" date.

Needs your OK on the new table (Q9)

9. Risks and open points

Compliance, needs an answer before design. A ranked "top funds" list, or a score shown next to funds the user does not own, can read as a recommendation. Whether that needs registration or specific disclaimers for us is a legal question, and I have not checked current SEBI rules. The Individual Fund spec already bans advice wording in generated text, and that rule would carry over. Please confirm who gives the compliance view. See Q4.
  • Cost spikes. Category pools reach 1,000+ rows for index funds. A cold open is fine for one fund and not fine for a list.
  • Misleading emptiness. A fund with no holdings file looks like a fund with no holdings. Every empty section needs its reason in words.
  • Score comparability. Score is a percentile within category, so it says nothing across categories. Lists that sort by score across categories would mislead.
  • Stale data hidden. Reads do not check price age. Every Explore number needs an "as of" date.
  • Scorer V2 on hold. Whatever score we show now is v1 (three components). Showing it prominently on 14,000 funds raises the stakes on a score you plan to replace.
  • Search overlap. Another session is designing page-wise search. Explore needs fund search. We should agree one endpoint, not build two.
  • Scope creep. AGENTS.md says do not gold-plate features that were not scoped. This feature is new scope and needs a PRD before code.
