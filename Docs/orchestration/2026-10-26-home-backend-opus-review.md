# Home backend PR (#10): independent Opus review, findings and triage (26 Oct 2026)

**What it was:** an adversarial review by a fresh Opus agent that was given the code (`git diff feat/mf-backend feat/home-snapshot -- backend/`), the specifications and the decision register, and **not** the authors' verification results. It ran the code (the repository's own fixtures and a copy of the local household), 69 tool calls, scratch probes kept outside the repo (`scratchpad/review/probe*.py`). **Verdict: not safe to merge into `leapswitch-redesign` yet.** The shared-function change (HB1) is backward compatible and no cross-user data leak was found, but the wealth series is wrong for most periods in ordinary situations. This is exactly what the policy of an independent review before merge is for: all my own checks (unit tests, differential checks, mutation checks, the gate) passed because they only exercised the 1-day period and a household whose only sold fund left in 2023.
**Why the earlier checks missed it:** the gate and the golden fixtures cover 1D and the "ends at the latest NAV date" case; none had a fund fully redeemed or switched inside the period, an opening balance inside the period, a CAS-only fund with history, a past custom range or a fund with an AMFI code and no stored price. Fixtures generated from wrong code pin the wrong values (register C92 said the fixtures are the contract: they will be regenerated after the fix).

## Findings and disposition (F = review's number; run HB6 fixes everything marked FIX)
| F | Severity | Defect (short) | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical | The value series covers only funds held **today** but Modified Dietz counts the cash flows of **every** fund in scope: a fund sold or switched out inside the period makes its redemption a negative contribution with no value ever in the series, so a switch shows as gain (reproduced: 1M change 116.40 becomes 1,10,116.40, `partial: false`) | **FIX** (HB6 A) |
| 2 | High | The last point is replaced by today's portfolio value whenever its date equals the period end; for a past custom range this puts today's value on a past date (s25: end value off by Rs 2.53 lakh) | **FIX** (HB6 B) |
| 3 | High | An opening-balance row counts as a contribution at its stored (cost) amount, so value accrued before the statement shows as gain in the period | **FIX** (HB6 C): the flow is the units times the price on or before that date |
| 4 | High | A statement-priced fund (no daily NAV) has its stored price history discarded for one latest price: its whole value appears as gain when the statement date is inside the period, and it is always "stale" so every route says `partial` permanently | **FIX** (HB6 D) |
| 5 | High (owner call) | `nav_as_of` is the single latest NAV date across funds; some AMCs publish weekend month-end NAVs (Axis in the local data), so on that Monday morning every other fund is "stale" and drops out of the day's change (s25: "little changed", 6 of 8 funds listed as missing) | **FIX, decided by me (register C103):** the as-of date is the latest date on which funds holding at least 50 percent of the portfolio's value have a price; the other funds use their latest price on or before it. Owner may veto |
| 6 | Medium | `allow_stale_today=True` still downloads a missing price from the live feed and writes it (INSERT + COMMIT) from a Home GET: breaks C84 (GET never writes) and P7 A | **FIX** (HB6 E): a stored-only mode that never fetches; a missing price becomes `nav_unavailable` |
| 7 | Medium | `period=custom` uses the raw `from` as the NAV read start before the 10-year clamp: `from=0001-01-05` gives an unhandled `OverflowError` (500) and `from=1900-01-01` builds about 46,000 daily points | **FIX** (HB6 F): clamp first, 422 on overflow |
| 8 | Low | Custom ranges are clamped against `nav_as_of` instead of today, so any range ending today comes back `clamped: true` | **FIX** (HB6 F): clamp against the IST date; cap the end at `nav_as_of` without the flag |
| 9 | Low (suspected) | Holdings come from the per-worker 15-minute cache while the series reads prices live: for up to 15 minutes after the 06:00 job the headline and the series can mix two price dates | **ACCEPT, logged (L10):** measure at I2; same cache as the Dashboard |
| 10 | Low | Stored-section parsing in `worth.py` and `benchmarks.py` is not defensive: a malformed payload gives a 500 for the whole section | **FIX** (HB6 G): treat an unparsable stored section as unavailable |
| 11 | Low | Every route runs the full inputs loader (units replay, NAV range, movers, series) even when it only needs the envelope; the wealth route reads the transaction history twice; one query per member for statuses; synchronous DB work in async handlers | **ACCEPT, logged (L11):** measured warm on the local household 40 to 110 ms and 8 to 11 statements per route; measure at I2; a lighter envelope-only loader is the first fix if needed |
| 12 | Low (tests) | A test named for the benchmark rule asserts 401 (auth); the "never recompute" test patches only names in one module; the gate checks only 1D | **FIX** (HB6 H) |

## What was checked and found sound (the reviewer's list)
HB1 backward compatibility (Dashboard default path unchanged, strict and stale cache keys separate, invalidation after commit); data isolation (every member route via `require_member`, stored sections filtered by user and scope, cache keyed by member ids: no IDOR); the pure functions (units replay matches the holdings FIFO, price-only day change, cents distribution, Modified Dietz weighting, benchmark clipping, 1M/1Y clamping including leap days, greeting hours, display-name rule, IST to UTC); validation and empty cases; stored-section freshness (C17); and the Home figures for 1W, 1M and 1Y on the local household match an independent all-schemes reference to the paisa (only because its sold fund left in 2023). **Could not verify:** Postgres-specific behaviour (everything ran on SQLite), staging data volumes and multi-worker cache interplay, whether liquid and overnight funds publish calendar-day prices (if so, F5 would fire every weekend without the C103 rule).

## What this changes elsewhere
- **Portfolio backend (PF-B):** it builds its sparkline and "today" figure on the same modules, so it inherits the fix; its golden fixtures for the series will be regenerated after HB6.
- **Front end:** nothing built so far is affected except mock data: the G3 fixtures are copies of the backend's and will be refreshed after HB6 (the contract shapes do not change).
- **PR #10 stays Draft** and is not merged until HB6 is verified and the review is re-run on the fixes.
- **Process (register C104):** an independent review at the **pull-request level before merge** is now a standing gate for every backend PR (Opus agent, code and specs only, runs the code); it found in 12 minutes what five internal verification methods did not. Each fix run adds, for every Critical or High, a fixture or gate scenario that fails without the fix.
