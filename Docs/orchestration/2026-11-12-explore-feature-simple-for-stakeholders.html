Explore: what we are planning

A plain-language overview for stakeholders. Updated 21 Nov 2026 with the stakeholder brief. Nothing is built yet. This page shows what Explore is, how it would look and feel, what comes in which release, and the decisions made and still open.

What changed on 21 Nov. We went through your Explore Tab brief item by item. New section 2A maps each item to what we have and plan. Screens S11 to S23 draw the brief’s fund research tabs, report card, change tracker, sector rotation, risk, flows, expenses, manager, news, scheme details, the seven-group home, search by manager and stock, and the watchlist, compare tray and Ask strip. Build order now has six releases. Nine new questions (Q17 to Q25) are in section 7. One finding needs a decision soon: how long we keep fund holdings history.
How to read it. Section 1 is the idea in one page. Section 2 is what a user can do, including what a new user sees before they add a statement. Section 3 shows the screens. Section 4 walks through a few journeys. Section 5 is the order we would build in. Section 6 is the care we take with wording and numbers. Section 7 lists the decisions, including one that needs a legal review.

1. The idea

Explore lets a signed-in user look beyond their own portfolio: see what other mutual funds exist, how they work and how they have behaved, and how each one fits what they already own.

In one paragraph. Today Unifolio shows the user the funds they already own. Explore adds a place to discover the rest of the market. A user can search any fund, manager or stock, browse by category or fund house, read plain-words guides, and open a research page for any fund with tabs for overview, portfolio and monthly changes, performance, risk, size and flows, manager, expenses, updates and scheme details, and how the fund overlaps with their own portfolio. Later, they can filter the market, put funds side by side, and replay how a fund would have done on an example investment. Every fund carries a score and a rank within its category, subject to a legal review (section 7).
IsIs not
A place to discover and learn about mutual fundsA place to buy funds. There is no “invest now” button.
For signed-in users only, for nowA public website section open to everyone
Mutual funds only for nowStocks, gold, bonds or real estate. Those are ideas for later and nothing is planned.

2A. Your brief, item by item

The 21 Nov brief (Explore Dashboard, nine-tab Fund Research Page, simple-view rules, extra features, data rules) compared with what we have and what we planned. The detailed document has the full table with the technical reasons.

The one finding that needs a decision now. The brief’s most important section, the stock change tracker, and the sector rotation and mix history need many months of fund holdings. Today the app deletes anything older than 6 months in its daily job. Each month deleted may not be recoverable, so we should decide how long to keep history (question Q18 below) before more months are lost.
How we found out. A read-only check of what the app stores, done on 21 Nov and reported to us, not independently re-read. Counts such as “about half of funds have holdings” are earlier measurements. Phases and sizes are our estimates.

6. The care we take

Explore shows funds the user does not own, scores them, and uses their holdings, so the wording and the numbers have to be careful.

  • No advice words. No “best”, “recommended”, “top pick”, “buy”, “avoid” or “must-have” anywhere. The score is named as data, not a verdict.
  • Every score and rank carries a methodology link and a disclaimer next to it: based on past data, not a forecast, not personalised advice. Direct and Regular plans are never ranked against each other.
  • Every number has its date beside it, and the past-performance disclaimer sits next to the data, not only in the footer.
  • Missing data is shown honestly. A fund with gaps gets a “Limited data” chip. A missing figure is a dash, never a zero. A fund under one year old shows no 3 or 5 year return.
  • One definition per category taken from SEBI’s own list, written once in plain words and reviewed line by line before it goes live.
  • Using the user’s holdings is stated up front. A short consent line tells the user that their holdings personalise Explore, before the portfolio features go live.
  • Portfolio wording stays descriptive. “18% overlap”, never “you should switch”.
  • A legal review of how we show scores and ranks happens before anything ranked goes live (section 7).
  • The standard risk sentence is on every Explore page and in the report: “Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully.”
  • Where we do not have data we say “Data unavailable”. We never show a made-up or guessed figure. Estimates, such as fund flows, are labelled as estimates.
  • The plain-words line and the report card use neutral, fixed wording taken from the scheme’s own product label and from comparison with similar funds. No suitability statements of our own.
  • Personal details about managers (photo, LinkedIn, education) appear only with permission and after checking.

⚠ Legal counsel must review this before launch

Option C is the most useful for discovery and also the one with the most regulatory exposure. We recommend that the company’s legal counsel reviews it and gives a written view before any score or rank is shown to users. Until then, the screens are built so that ranks and scores stay hidden (as drawn on the category page).

  • The core question. We found no SEBI text saying a formula-based score or ranked list of mutual funds is “investment advice”, and none saying it is not. The Investment Advisers rules require registration for anyone in the business of giving investment advice, and they have no clear exception for general, formula-based information.
  • Peers are split. One well-known rating provider operates under SEBI registrations. Others publish ratings with a “not advice” disclaimer, and one says it is not registered. Our position is closest to the second group.
  • The sharpest indirect risk is a partner risk. A SEBI rule (Regulation 16A) stops regulated entities, such as fund houses and distributors, from associating with anyone who gives advice or claims returns or performance without permission. It affects partnerships, referral income and data feeds more than the display itself.
  • “Best in category” and “top 10” style pages are the riskiest surface. That is why the wording rules in section 6 matter.
  • Questions for counsel. Is a formula-based score or rank “investment advice”? Does the exception for content widely available to the public cover a signed-in app? Does a free product with partnerships or a paid tier count as “consideration”? Are fund units “securities” for the adviser and analyst rules? How does Regulation 16A apply to our partners? Does showing past returns count as a “claim of returns or performance”?
  • What we do meanwhile. Use data wording only; publish a methodology page; put the disclaimer next to every score and rank; keep Direct and Regular separate; no invest buttons, no affiliate links; and build it so scores and ranks can be switched off per page without rework.

This is our research summary, not legal advice, and it has not been independently verified. The sources are in the detailed document.
