#FindingWhy it matters
F1Dates are written by six helpers with different results: formatDate “12 Oct 2026”; formatExactDate “12 October 2026”; holdingsDate (a browser formatter, which may print “Sept” for September; not checked); formatDayMonth “12 Oct”; formatAsOf “as of 12 Oct”; and two functions both named formatMonthYear, one printing “October 2026” and one “Oct 2026”.The same kind of date can read three ways on three screens, and a developer cannot tell which helper is the standard.

What the user reads today, and the three policies (decided: Q1 B, two tiers)

Policy

Made-up dates. “Event” dates (a transaction, a SIP due date, “managing since”, first and last investment) are real dates of things that happened; they stay exact in every policy. The policy is only about data as-of labels.

3. Category average: the legend and the “i” (item 9)

A popover on the category card that shows the legend and explains the calculation. The words are written now; the numbers in them are checked against Batch A’s verdict before they ship.

What the card has today (read in code)

  • A one-sentence tooltip on the card title: “The average of funds in the same category and plan type, weighted by fund size. Each fund needs at least 3 years of history.”
  • Under the rows: “Category average covers 31 funds (1Y), 31 (3Y), 24 (5Y).” and the legend line “Filled dot: your fund. Hollow dot: category average. Green means ahead; red means behind.”
  • Each period row (1Y, 3Y, 5Y) shows a coloured gap such as +1.2% over 4.6% vs 3.4%. Those are the “three values” you described in Batch A.

The proposed “i” popover

State

Made-up fund and numbers; laptop view. The popover opens when the icon is hovered or focused and stays open when it is clicked; Escape or a click outside closes it.

Draft copy (plain words; final after Batch A’s verdict):

How to read this

  • Filled dot is your fund. Hollow dot is the category average.
  • The number above the dots is the gap: your fund’s return minus the category average. Green means ahead, red means behind.
  • The second line shows the two returns: your fund against the category.
  • There is one row for each period: 1 year, 3 years and 5 years.

How the average is worked out

  • We take every fund in the same SEBI category and the same plan (Direct with Direct, Regular with Regular).
  • Only funds with at least 3 years of history count, in every period.
  • Each fund counts in proportion to its size (its average assets under management), so a large fund moves the average more than a small one. A fund with no size on record is left out of the weighting.
  • If no fund in the category has a size, we show a plain average and say so.

Good to know

  • Categories with fewer than 5 funds are marked as small, so treat the average with care.
  • Returns are based on prices as on 12 Nov 2026 (the date follows the policy: Q1 B).

4. P/E and P/B: one “i” instead of source pills (item 16)

Remove the pills from every value and put one “i” next to the fund name that explains where each figure came from.

What the table does today (read in code)

  • Each fund row has a P/E cell and a P/B cell. Each shows the figure, a source pill (“Factsheet”, “Factsheet fwd” or “NSE/BSE”) with a hover note “Basis: trailing earnings. Method: harmonic mean.”, and a small range bar with a Nifty 500 tick.
  • A fourth column, “As on”, shows one date or two (P/E and P/B) per fund.

Where each figure comes from (read in code)

Source on screen todayWhat it really isWhat the popover says
Factsheet / Factsheet fwdThe fund’s own P/E or P/B printed on its monthly factsheet (a reference month-end). “fwd” means forward earnings. The method may be stated (for example harmonic mean) or not.“Printed on the fund’s factsheet for September 2026, based on forward earnings.”
NSE/BSECalculated by us from the fund’s published holdings: each stock’s P/E comes from the exchanges’ daily files (NSE, and BSE for stocks only listed there); P/B uses book value from company filings. The fund’s figure is a harmonic mean of its stocks’ ratios, shown only when at least 60% of its equity has a usable ratio. The date is the oldest stock-ratio date.“Worked out from the fund’s published holdings. Stock ratios come from NSE and BSE daily data, combined as a harmonic mean. Covers 92% of the fund’s equity. Ratios as on 12 Nov 2026.”

The proposed “i” (one per fund)

  • Sits right after the fund name, so it is easy to find and does not crowd the figures.
  • The popover has two short parts, P/E and P/B, each with: the source, the basis (trailing or forward earnings; trailing book value), the method (harmonic mean or weighted average), the coverage (computed figures only), and the date in the policy’s form.
  • If a figure is withheld (not enough data), the popover says why in the same plain words the cell shows today.
  • The line “Indicators, not a recommendation.” stays, inside the popover.
  • The “As on” column goes: each fund’s date moves into its “i” and the page strip carries the usual one (item 11, my default; tell me if you want the column kept).
Depends on the fact-sheet fixes (C3). Your manager is changing how fact-sheet figures are loaded (manual and automatic). If a new source such as “loaded by hand” appears, the popover needs a sentence for it. The words live in one source-to-sentence map, so a new source is one line, and I check the final list when the fixes land.
