In plain words

Short answer: no. The unfinished checklist did not cause the block. The block comes from a record on our server, not from anything in your browser.

  1. What you saw. In Profile, "Choose who will be Head of Family" showed every member greyed out with "Finish or cancel the statement you are importing first." and Next was disabled, although every detail was filled in.
  2. What really happened. The moment you press Request from CAMS, our server saves a small record: "a statement was requested for this person, waiting for the CAMS email". It stays for about 2 days. The Head of Family check looks at the whole family, sees any such record, and blocks everyone. It does not care who the record belongs to.
  3. The checklist is only a note-to-self. Its ticks, its "0 of 5 done" and its open or closed window live only in your own browser. The server never hears about them. So ticking all five boxes, or closing it, changes nothing on the server.
  4. Finishing everything would not have helped. Even if you had ticked all five, clicked "I have the file", closed the checklist and uploaded the statement, the "waiting" record would still be there. We found that nothing in the code closes it when the file is imported. It ends only when (a) you press "Cancel request" (which stops working after a page reload), (b) about 2 days pass and the nightly clean-up runs, or (c) you request again (that restarts the 2 days).
  5. What we will do. (A) The Head of Family check must never look at waiting or abandoned requests. (B) A waiting request must be closed when its file is imported, and old stuck ones cleaned up. (C) A waiting request must be visible, with a Cancel that always works. (D) The checklist becomes a pure reading aid, connected to nothing, and easier to see. Each part is its own piece below, with its own tests.

The plan: four parts, each with its own tests (decided: all four, in order A, B, C, D)

Part A · Head of Family never looks at requests

A waiting, abandoned, failed or old import can never stop a change. The household-wide check is removed from the eligibility list, from the start-code step, from the start step, from the dialog and from the last step (which re-runs the same check). The other eleven reasons stay exactly as they are.

  • An unfinished statement review (uploaded, not confirmed, up to about 65 minutes): decided (D1 C) it never blocks. It is discarded inside the change together with its PAN claim, and the completion notice says so (mockup 6 below). The change already signs everyone out, so nothing half-attributed survives.
  • A statement being saved at the exact moment (D2 A): the last step answers with the plain sentence "A statement is being saved. Try again in a minute." in a calm, designed notice (mockup 7), the codes stay valid, and nothing is lost.
  • Not in this work (D4 A): abandoned review rows still stay open until the next import call; no daily job cleans them. Noted as a follow-up.
  • D3 A: nobody remembers a reason for the old rule beyond caution, so the evidence from step R6 decides whether discarding is safe.

Part B · Close the waiting record when it has done its job

  • Q6 A: when a statement is imported, close the waiting request of that member only. Other members' requests stay.
  • Pressing Cancel request closes it too. Reset checklist does not (Q3 A).
  • Q7 A: a one-off script, dry run first (counts only), closes requests that have a later confirmed import or are past 48 hours. A data fix, no schema change (confirm in R7).

Part C · A waiting request is visible and cancellable

  • Q5 A: Add data asks the server "is a request waiting for this member?" and cancels by the id the server returns. Works on any device and after any reload.
  • A small "CAMS request waiting" card shows who it is for, the time left, and a Cancel button (mockups 2 and 5).
  • Q4 B: the Head of Family dialog shows one quiet line, no button, when a request waits: "A CAMS request for Rohan is still waiting. It does not stop this change." (mockup 4).

Part D · The checklist becomes a reading aid, and easier to see

  • Decouple. The checklist opens from its own control and from Request (still the first thing in the click, because of the browser rule). The page never waits for it; its notes never choose the Add data screen; coming back to the tab never changes the screen because of it; Reset and Close only touch the checklist; a failure shows a message and nothing else breaks.
  • The page chooses its screen from the server: a waiting request shows the waiting card; otherwise the usual choice.
  • Make it visible. A proper status strip (progress bar, current step, Reopen) replaces the plain "0 of 5 done" line and the quiet "closed" banner; the floating checklist gets a clearer header and a progress bar (mockups 2 and 3).
  • Q3 A: "Start over" becomes Reset checklist.
  • Q2 (your note): the "Continue without it" line before the Request button becomes a clearly visible control, not a small link at the end of a sentence (mockup 5). Today it is a 12.5px green link tucked inside a green-tinted note, hidden on phones, and one way only: once clicked, it cannot be undone without reloading.

Order and size: Part A is small and independent (back end plus a few lines in the dialog) and unblocks stage 9. B and C touch the CAMS request flow; D is the front-end clean-up and shares files with D-2. Each part is valuable alone and ships in order A, B, C, D.
