5. News: what we researched and what we recommend

A plain-language summary for decision-makers. It covers what we needed, what we looked at, what we found, what we recommend and why, and what we ruled out. The full detailed research is kept in the appendix at the end of this section. All testing was done on 21 and 22 October 2026. This is research, not legal advice.

The recommendation in one minute

  • What we are building: a news card on the Home screen showing 1 to 3 headlines that matter to what the user holds (their funds, gold, interest rates, banking and so on).
  • Our pick (decided 22 Oct): use GDELT (a free public index of news articles from around the world) as the only source for now. No paid news service and no comparison trial. The official SEBI and RBI feeds and any paid service are possible later options, not part of this plan.
  • Why: it is the only free route that worked reliably from a server and also has a licence that allows commercial use. Nearly every other free route says in its own terms that it is for personal, non-commercial use only.
  • How the card updates (your rules): only when a headline specific to what the user holds arrives, or when their holdings change. Never on a timer. A specific item stays for one day, then other news fills in. See 5.9.
  • Cost to start: no licence fee. About 18 to 21 developer-days. One new background job and no new software packages. On the VM no new servers; on AWS production one new scheduled-task entry. Running cost is a few dollars a month at most (an estimate, not priced). See 5.10.
  • What we need from you and your manager: (1) agree this direction; (2) a lawyer's review before launch; (3) a one-to-two-week dark launch (card hidden) to review matches on real holdings before we turn it on.

5.1 What we needed

  • 1 to 3 relevant headlines per user per day, ideally about the user's mutual funds, otherwise general market news.
  • Legally safe for a commercial product that is free at its core and may have a paid tier later.
  • Reuse our current code and servers; add a new background job only if truly needed.
  • One or two sources, free if possible. No scraping of sites that forbid it.

5.3 What we found

  1. Most free news feeds are not licensed for a commercial product. Publisher feeds, Google and Bing all say "personal, non-commercial use". That rules out the easy choices.
  2. Google News is the most convenient source and the least usable. It works well, but its own feed text and robots.txt forbid what we would do, and its links cannot be turned into publisher links.
  3. A paid service is the clean legal route, but it is not part of this plan. It would cost about EUR 50 a month (GNews), flat whether we have 1,000 or 100,000 users. It licenses its own service, not the publishers' copyright on headlines. It stays a possible later option.
  4. GDELT works, in file form. In a full 24-hour test: 96 of 96 files downloaded (506 MB), 118,025 articles worldwide, 4,707 from Indian news sites, 4,582 distinct headlines.
  5. GDELT is thin per stock but plentiful by topic. HDFC Bank 14 headlines a day, Infosys 7, ICICI Bank 1. But gold 51, RBI and interest rates 46, the market 38, banking 30, mutual funds in general 20. So the card should match topics (gold, rates, banking, the user's fund categories and fund houses), not only stocks. News about one specific fund is rare (3 a day).
  6. Official feeds are a safe floor, not a news source. SEBI and RBI work and cost nothing, but are mostly regulatory.
  7. Headline plus link looks low-risk in Indian law; wording matters. A card saying "affecting your portfolio" can start to look like personalised advice, so we plan a neutral label and a disclaimer.
  8. No new infrastructure is needed. One new job script, three cron lines, one new table, no new software packages.

5.4 The recommended option, and why

Source: GDELT raw files only, for now. No paid service and no comparison trial.

Pros

  • Free, with a licence that allows commercial use (credit and link required)
  • Real links to the publisher's page
  • Good mix of Indian business sites; about 4,600 headlines a day
  • Downloads were 100% reliable in the test
  • No account, no key, no per-user cost
  • No new servers or packages
  • The source is a plug-in, so another source could be added later if ever needed

Cons and costs

  • Thin coverage per stock; topics fill the gap but are looser than "about your holding"
  • Almost no news about one specific fund or ETF (3 a day each)
  • Its search API is blocked for us, so only the raw files work
  • Heavy downloads: about 0.5 GB a day, VM limits untested
  • No service guarantee; the newest files can be missing for a while
  • Titles only, no snippet; we build and keep the matching dictionary
  • Publisher copyright on headlines is still an open legal question (same for any source)

Why this one: it is free, it works, it has a commercial-use licence for its own data, and it keeps the paid option open. The alternatives are either not licensed for commercial use, blocked, or cost money before we know we need them.

How it would work, in five lines:

  1. Twice a day (07:30 and 19:30 IST) one background job downloads GDELT's latest files.
  2. It keeps only articles from Indian business news sites (plus a few global business sites, used only for foreign companies a user actually holds) and checks each headline against a list of names and topics: stocks, fund houses, fund categories, gold, rates, banking and so on.
  3. Matching headlines are saved in one new table, kept for 14 days.
  4. When a user opens Home, the server scores those headlines against what that user holds (using look-through, asset-class and fund data we already have) and picks the top 1 to 3.
  5. The card shows the headline, source, time and a link out, with a GDELT credit and a short disclaimer. If nothing specific exists, it shows general market, rates and regulator headlines from GDELT, or hides.

5.5 Why we are not choosing the others

  • Google and Bing news feeds: free and relevant, but their own terms say personal, non-commercial use only, and Google disallows the path in robots.txt.
  • Publisher feeds: ET and BusinessLine forbid commercial use; Mint and CNBC-TV18 give no licence; Moneycontrol and Business Standard block our server.
  • Paid services: the clear legal route, but you decided on GDELT only for now, so no paid service and no comparison trial. They remain a possible later option.
  • BSE, NSE, AMFI and RBI as the main source: blocked, licensed, or restricted in their terms; they carry company filings and circulars, not news.
  • Wires, Brave, SerpApi, Google Alerts, scraped datasets: terms bar redistribution or storage, or the legal position is the same as Google News RSS.
  • Publisher licences: the best legal standing, but prices are not public and it means negotiating with each. We will email them in parallel without blocking the build.
  • Cards from our own data: clean, but they are insights, not news, and overlap cards already on Home (what moved today, the benchmark comparison, Worth a look).

5.6 Why we ruled out reverse engineering

  • The sites have said no. Moneycontrol blocks automated access (403 even on its robots.txt and terms page); Groww, Yahoo and ET disallow their internal data paths in robots.txt. Using them would mean going round a block, which we will not do.
  • It would break. Undocumented routes exist for the site's own app and change without notice. A news card that silently empties out is a production problem.
  • Highest legal exposure of anything we looked at (IT Act sections 43 and 66, copyright, contract; unverified and needing a lawyer). We would store content from a site that told us not to take it, in a commercial product.
  • It does not remove the underlying question about publisher copyright on headlines. It only adds risk and breakage.
  • What is fine instead: reading public pages and terms, and using feeds or bulk data that the owner offers for reuse, as GDELT does. For anything else the proper route is to ask the site for permission or a licence.

Minimal pipeline proposed by the research

Indian legal landscape (all UNVERIFIED unless marked; not legal advice)

  • Titles: Krishika Lulla v Shyam Vithalrao Devkatta (Supreme Court, 15 Oct 2015): no copyright in the title of a literary work (secondary sources). Long headlines and snippets are riskier than short ones.
  • Facts versus expression: ANI v OpenAI (Delhi High Court, interim order of 24 Jul 2026) applied the idea and expression test, said facts and events belong to nobody, and found storing articles for training prima facie fair dealing. ANI has appealed, listed 8 Dec. It is interim and not binding on a trial.
  • Wholesale copying: ANI v RSY News (Delhi High Court, 2026) held wholesale copying of news videos is not fair dealing. Implication: headline plus short snippet plus link is low-risk; full text is high-risk. Whether a tracker's use fits the "reporting current events" purpose is uncertain.
  • Aggregators: the Competition Commission ordered an investigation of Google in January 2022 on a publishers' complaint, including whether snippets reduce referral traffic; no outcome found. The status of the 2026 Digital Competition Bill is unknown. No Indian ruling on aggregator snippets found.
  • Scraping and website terms: no Indian precedent found on whether terms against scraping are enforceable. Prefer licensed or official feeds, honour robots.txt and stated terms. RBI's and AMFI's explicit linking clauses are contractual statements to respect or get waived.
  • IT Act section 79 and the IT Rules 2021: whether a curated, personalised headline card counts as passive intermediary activity is unresolved. It may look more like publishing (the agent's inference).
  • SEBI adviser and research-analyst rules: advice "widely available to the public" is excluded from investment advice. A card saying "news affecting your fund" is personalised, so with any buy, sell or hold tone, or a call-to-action such as "Worth a look", it risks being treated as advice or research. Safer: neutral wording such as "In the news", no sentiment, no ranking by opportunity, and a disclaimer. The agent did not research the wording used by Indian apps or the mutual-fund advertising code.
  • DPDP Act 2023: the rules were notified; core duties start 13 May 2027. Match headlines inside our own backend, never send holdings to vendors, and cover the use in the privacy notice. Counsel to confirm consent or notice wording.

Safe-pattern checklist

  • Prefer official feeds with open reuse and licensed APIs. No scraping of sites that block bots or forbid it.
  • Store only title, source, URL, date and at most 150 to 200 characters of publisher-supplied description. No body text, no images, short retention.
  • Display title with attribution (publisher and date) and a link out to the publisher. No framing or caching of pages.
  • Neutral label and a static disclaimer, for example: "Headlines are from third-party sources, shown for information, not investment advice or research."
  • Log which source produced each item. Takedown process (contact, removal within 24 to 48 hours), a per-source and a global kill switch.
  • Record, per source, the date of the robots.txt and terms check, a snapshot of the terms, and any written permission. Ask in writing: RBI and AMFI (links), SEBI (reuse), BSE and NSE (licence).

  2. Is a headline plus up to 200 characters plus a link within fair dealing, or non-infringing as unprotected title and facts?
  3. Are we an intermediary under section 79, and what takedown process do the IT Rules 2021 require?
  4. Do RBI's and AMFI's linking and framing clauses bind us for links taken from their RSS, and how do we obtain a waiver?
  5. What consent or notice wording does DPDP need for personalised news, and how does it relate to 13 May 2027?
  6. Can we republish summaries of AMC addenda and link to the AMC PDFs?

Headline

No new infrastructure. One cron line, one job script, one service package, one new table (news_items), one setting (news_enabled), two small API routes. No per-user table, no worker, no scheduler.

How a news job plugs into what exists

  • Cron: deploy/leapswitch/crontab.leapswitch uses CRON_TZ=Asia/Kolkata and lines of the form $RUN name scripts/jobs/file.py. The memory checker runs at 05:40 to 05:55 and 06:00 to 06:35 every 5 minutes (threshold 1,000 MB), so the news job should avoid that window. The agent proposed 30 7,13,19 * * *; with your choice of twice a day (E2) it becomes 30 7,19 * * *.
  • Wrapper: run-job.sh runs docker compose run --rm -T backend python ... and, on a nonzero exit, emails an alert through SES. It has no lock, no timeout and no retry. Important: log markers such as the existing *_ALERT lines only alert on AWS through CloudWatch. On the VM, an alert fires only if the job exits nonzero, so the news job must exit 1 on total failure or staleness.
  • Job pattern: same shape as refresh_benchmark_daily.py (a thin script around an async main with a database session); plain db.commit() is fine in a cron job. HTTP uses the same httpx pattern as the benchmark client. RapidFuzz 3.14.5 and httpx 0.28.1 are already pinned in requirements; feedparser and tenacity are not.
  • Locking: add flock -n /tmp/unifolio-news.lock in front (new to this crontab) or a Postgres advisory lock inside the job.
  • Retries and limits: 10-second request timeout; at most 2 retries on 429, 5xx or timeouts with exponential backoff and jitter; 1.0 to 1.5 seconds between requests; honour Retry-After; circuit breaker: stop the run after 5 consecutive failures or any 429, keep the last good set, exit nonzero; a 15-minute wall-clock cap.
  • Memory: a fresh one-off container; expected well under 150 MB (an estimate to measure on the VM).
  • Failure rules: exit 1 if zero queries succeeded, the breaker tripped, or the newest fetch is older than 30 hours. Partial failure exits 0 with a logged warning. If news_enabled is false, the job logs and exits 0.

Ties: relevance, then newer, then id. Drop clickbait or tip lists. API, kill switch, tests, monitoring, rollout

  • Routes: GET /household/aggregate/news and GET /household-members/{id}/news, next to the existing scoped routes. Response: status (ok, stale, empty, disabled), generated and fetch times, holdings-as-of, coverage, and up to three items with title, url, publisher, time, reason (stock, amc, fund, sector, market) and the related entity with its exposure note. No images, no body text. Cache 15 minutes per user and scope.
  • Front-end states: ok shows up to 3 rows; empty, disabled or a network error hides the card and never blocks Home; stale (over 36 hours) shows the items with a muted "Updated" date and hides after 72 hours.
  • Kill switch: a news_enabled setting in config next to the existing scenario release gate, checked in the job and the route (the route returns "disabled" with HTTP 200). A restart is needed to change an environment setting, which is acceptable for a kill switch.

E. What GDELT is, and what the tests showed

What it is

GDELT is a public project that logs news articles from around the world. It publishes a search API and, every 15 minutes, three raw files: events, mentions and the "GKG" (global knowledge graph), which lists each article's source site, link, topic tags and mentioned organisations. The GKG file also carries the page title.

Licence (VERIFIED by the round 2 agent, gdeltproject.org/about.html): "available for unlimited and unrestricted use for any academic, commercial, or governmental use of any kind without fee ... You may redistribute, rehost, republish, and mirror any of the GDELT datasets in any form. However, any use or redistribution of the data must include a citation to the GDELT Project and a link". This covers GDELT's own data. It does not override a publisher's copyright on its headlines; that question is the same as for any aggregator, and the legal research rated a headline plus link as low-risk. It is research, not legal advice.

  • The round 2 agent got one successful response and then about 8 consecutive 429s. So the API is unreliable from here. Whether the limit is per IP address or shared is unknown.

Caveats

  • Headline counts include the same story on several sites; distinct stories are likely 30 to 50 percent fewer.

How widening changes the pipeline design

  • No query planner. With GDELT files there are no per-stock queries and no request budget to rotate. The job downloads the files published since its last run (about 48 files, 250 MB, for a twice-daily run), reads each in a streaming way, keeps only rows from Indian news sites, and stores only headlines that match at least one entity. My 24-hour test took about 7 minutes including a deliberate 1.2-second pause between files, so about half that for one run's worth. Memory per file is about 30 MB when unpacked.
  • More entity types. The matcher looks for: stock, fund house, fund category (small cap, flexi cap, ELSS, liquid, gilt and so on), asset class or commodity (equity, debt, gold, silver, crude), rates and macro (RBI, repo, inflation, bond yields, rupee, budget), sector and regulator (SEBI, AMFI). Weights per user come from data we already have: the look-through asset-class split, each fund's category from the schemes table, and the funds' top holdings.
  • Scoring extension. The earlier scoring formula gains two entity kinds: asset class (relevance rises with the user's share in that class, for example gold at 10 percent of the portfolio) and category (the share of value in that category). Reason chips on the card would say what matched ("Gold", "Rates", "Banking stocks", "Your fund house").
  • Table and retention unchanged. One news_items table, 14 days, reference data not per user; entities stored as tagged keys. Expected rows per day: a few hundred matched headlines (an estimate).
  • Kill switch and adapter unchanged. GDELT is the only source for now. The source is a plug-in, so another could be added later without touching the rest.

What we actually use from GDELT

  • Only the GKG file (YYYYMMDDHHMMSS.gkg.csv.zip), one every 15 minutes. Not the events file, not the mentions file, not the search API, not BigQuery.
  • Five fields per article (positions verified by my test: each row had 27 tab-separated columns): the capture time (column 1, the 15-minute batch time, in UTC), the source site (column 3), the article URL (column 4), GDELT's topic tags (column 8) and the organisations it found (column 14). The article title sits inside the "extras" column (column 26) as a PAGE_TITLE tag; every row in my 24-hour sample had one. Whether the batch time is a good stand-in for the publication time is to confirm in the build.
  • What we keep: title (HTML entities decoded), URL, source site, time, and the entities our matcher found. No article text, no images, and nothing about people.
  • Citation: GDELT's licence requires a citation and a link back to the project wherever the data is used. See E14.

Data model: reuse first

  • Reuse check: none of the existing tables can hold third-party headlines. scheme_holdings, stock_master, schemes, disclosure_batches and the analytics tables are inputs only. The analytics tables are per-user precomputed sections; using them would create a per-user news store and tie news freshness to the 06:30 recompute. A JSON file in object storage would avoid a migration but loses atomic insert-or-ignore, de-duplication and retention queries, and complicates tests.
  • So one table, news_items, shared by all users: id, source (here "gdelt"), publisher (the site, mapped to a display name), title, url, published_at, fetched_at, title_hash, and an entities column (JSON, with the JSONB variant on Postgres, as DisclosureBatch.parse_errors does). Unique on (source, title_hash); index on published_at; no foreign keys; 14 days retention purged by the same job.
  • No per-user table. Relevance is computed when the user opens Home, from data we already hold.
  • No "last file processed" table, because of the overlapping window above.

The route and the card

  • Routes: GET /household/aggregate/news (family scope) and GET /household-members/{member_id}/news (one member), the same naming as the existing dashboard routes. The member route checks ownership with require_member, so another user's member id returns the same "not found" the other routes do.
  • Response: a status (ok, stale, empty, disabled), the generated time and the latest fetch time, and up to three items: title, link, site name, time, and a reason chip (stock, fund house, category, asset class, rates, sector or market) with an exposure note only when allowed (E9). No images and no body text. A disabled switch answers with status "disabled" and HTTP 200, so the front end just hides the card.
  • Caching: about 15 minutes per user and scope, in the same spirit as the existing holdings cache.
  • Front end (web and mobile): a card in the Home layout that never blocks the page; on any error or empty state it hides; reason chips replace the mockup's "Sector news" and "Company results" labels; the thumbnail slot uses our own icons (E3).

Running it: memory, time, bandwidth, failure

  • Memory: files are processed one at a time. In my test an unpacked file was 10 to 23 MB and parsing a 1,678-row file took 0.23 seconds, so memory stays well under the 1,000 MB threshold the morning checker uses (a one-off container; the backend service in the compose file has no memory limit set, while the worker has 3 GB). A real figure comes from the VM dry run.
  • Time: the full 96-file test took about 7 minutes including a deliberate 1.2-second pause per file, so one run's worth is a few minutes.
  • Bandwidth: about 250 MB to 500 MB per run depending on the window; about 0.5 GB to 1 GB a day. The VM's limits are unknown (E13).
  • Failure rules: missing files are skipped; the job keeps whatever it stored before; it never purges when no file could be fetched; non-zero exit on total failure, so the existing email alert fires. A partial failure exits 0 with a warning in the log.
  • Time zone: cron runs in IST, GDELT names are UTC; the job converts. Titles and times shown to users are converted to IST.
  • Clean-up of text: titles contain HTML entities (for example an em dash written as a numeric entity appeared in one headline), so decode them, trim site-name suffixes, and skip titles in scripts other than English (E16).

Testing (the repo's rules apply: affected tests only, and no live network in tests)

  • No live HTTP: the repo's test plugin forbids real HTTP transports, so the downloader is tested with httpx's mock transport and a trimmed real GKG slice kept under backend/tests/fixtures/news/ (rows from my 24-hour download; the saved titles also supply a ready-made source for a hand-labelled test set).
  • Unit tests: parsing and the Indian-site filter; window and 404 handling; alias dictionary built from in-memory rows; matcher precision on a labelled set of about 150 real headlines (hard cases: Titan, Trent, Eternal, Tata, HDFC Life versus HDFC Bank, Axis, Kotak, Adani, Bajaj, the toddler "10-year-old" false match); scorer determinism and diversity; de-duplication.

  • API tests: family and member scope, another user's member id, the disabled switch, exposure note hidden below 50 percent coverage, and a reconciliation check against the synthetic CAS set so a shown exposure equals the look-through row.

Changes caused by your update rules (22 Oct)

  • Scoring changes. The earlier design faded older items steadily (a 24-hour half-life), which makes the card's order shift just because time passes. That is removed. Items are ranked by how specific the match is and then by publication time. Each headline is tagged specific (a held stock above a threshold, a held fund house, a held fund category, an asset class above a threshold) or general (the market, rates, SEBI, sectors the user does not specifically hold).
  • Selection. Show up to 3 specific headlines published in the last 24 hours. If there are fewer than 3, fill with general market, SEBI and RBI items. General items never replace a specific item and never count as an update.
  • Cache and holdings change. The response is cached briefly (about 15 minutes) and the cache key includes the newest stored headline and the member's "data version", which is the time of the latest confirmed import (_data_version in snapshots.py). A new import therefore changes the key at once. A member merge changes the set of member ids and also clears the holdings cache. One caveat: the holdings cache is per server process (there are 3 workers), so the news cache would be per process too, which is fine for a 15-minute lifetime.
  • Job windows. With polling every 3 hours, the job reads about the last 5 hours of files per run, plus one 26-hour catch-up a day. Without that change, a 26-hour window on every run would mean about 4 GB a day.
  • Front end. The card refetches when the user returns to the app or tab (at most every 5 minutes) and on pull-to-refresh or a refresh control. There is no "New" badge (you decided against it).
  • Not changed. Direct stocks do not exist yet; stock relevance still comes from fund look-through. The matching is built so direct holdings can be added when stock tracking arrives.

Risks to keep in view

  • News licensing. Google News RSS and Bing RSS are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only, and Google's robots.txt disallows /rss; ET and BusinessLine forbid commercial RSS use. GDELT's licence covers its own data, not the publishers' copyright. Needs legal review before production.
  • News wording. A card personalised to holdings, with any buy, sell or hold tone, risks being treated as advice or research under SEBI rules. Neutral label, no sentiment, disclaimer (E8).
  • GDELT as the free source. No SLA; the search API returns 429; the newest listed files can 404 for a while; coverage of single stocks is thin and measured over one busy day only; its licence covers its own data, not the publishers' copyright. Mitigations: files only, files at least an hour old with retry, topic matching beyond stocks, a one-to-two-week dark launch on real holdings, a kill switch (E12, E13).
  • Blocked from the server. The tests ran from an Indian home connection. Cloud/VM IPs may get 403 or 429. Only a test from the VM settles it.
  • Numbers that disagree. Headline, graph, contributors and the summary sentence must reconcile. The plan includes explicit reconciliation tests.
  • Stale inputs. Holdings are as old as the last statement, disclosures up to about 40 days old, NAV one day old. Every number needs an honest "as of".
  • Shell change. Moving the whole app to a new navigation touches every screen. Step 1 is the riskiest and gets the most testing.
  • Benchmark history. The daily benchmark job keeps only the last 10 years (known item in DEFERRED_FEATURES.md); long custom ranges would silently exclude older flows.
