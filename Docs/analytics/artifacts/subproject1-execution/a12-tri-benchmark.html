Sub-project 1 · Attribute 12 · 2nd in run order

TRI benchmark sourcing

Stores the Total Return Index (dividends reinvested) next to today's price-only index for all 4 benchmarks, and re-keys the cache, freshness check and upsert so the two series can't collide. No new screen. TRI becomes the series every fund-vs-index comparison uses, starting with the existing Analytics benchmark section.

6 tasks · 5 planned + 1 added 9 Oct 1 migration (expected 0033) 0 new tables · 1 new column, wider primary key 0 routes · 0 new screens 0 new infra · +4 requests/day 1 Codex run

Decided 9 Oct: TRI for every comparison

Any calculation that compares a fund or portfolio with an index uses TRI, because NAVs already include reinvested dividends (SEBI has required TRI benchmarks since 2018). The price series stays in the table as raw data only. The TRI series is never displayed itself, but numbers computed from it are.

  • Task 6 (added): benchmark.py's two lookups pass return_type=TRI.
  • Older TRI (decided 9 Oct): the daily job asks for TRI from 1 Jan 1990, so scenarios back to 1995 get a benchmark. No separate script. Cost: under $0.01 a month; no effect on page speed or other jobs (resolved card 2).
  • No mixing: both ends of a window come from TRI. When TRI is missing there's no benchmark, never a quiet fallback to price.

Why

The bug this attribute exists to fix

A fund's NAV already includes reinvested dividends. Comparing it with a price-only index understates the benchmark every year. Adding TRI rows sounds simple, but every existing lookup in nse_indices_client.py is keyed by index name alone:

The price job has run daily for weeks, so almost every TRI row would be silently dropped. Five places have to move to (index, return_type): _upsert_index_history, _cached_date_bounds, the _fetched_from cache, ensure_index_history_fresh and get_index_level_on_or_before.

2 · Old scenarios need TRI from before 2016

was: Fix
The problem

Why 10 years today: the window came from the commit that first created the job scripts (9fe21fe, 3 Sep). It wasn't recorded as a decision anywhere. It looks like a reasonable-sounding default, not a requirement. Two facts matter here:

  • The job already downloads the whole window every morning. With fresh_within=0, "today" is never covered, so ensure_index_history_fresh fetches [start, today] in full each day and inserts only the new dates. Today that's 10 years × 4 indices daily.
  • Older history is already fetched on demand. benchmark.py:111 asks for history from the household's first transaction. A portfolio that started in 2008 triggers a download back to 2008 during its analytics recompute. That costs time in that recompute and needs NSE up at that moment.
Measured live today (TRI endpoint, start date 1 Jan 1990): one request per index returns all of it, in 0.2–0.4 s and about 0.8–1.1 MB:
IndexTRI startsRows
Nifty 5001 Jan 19957,873
Nifty 5030 Jun 19996,785
Nifty LargeMidcap 2501 Apr 20055,338
Nifty Midcap 1501 Apr 20055,338

One-off or daily? The Task 7 I proposed earlier was a one-off script. But the measurements make a script unnecessary. If the daily job simply asks for TRI from 1990, it already re-downloads its whole window each morning, so it fills the old years on day one and keeps them complete from then on. What else that fixes: (1) benchmark comparisons for portfolios older than 10 years no longer download history in the middle of a user's recompute; (2) deleted or missing rows are refilled the next morning. What it costs: about 4 MB and roughly 1–2 seconds of downloads per day instead of about 1.3 MB.

Fix chosen

B. Widen the daily job: TRI from 1 Jan 1990. Price stays at 10 years, since it's raw data only now.

It's a one-constant change instead of a new script and a manual run. It's self-healing, it covers every scenario from 1995 onward, and the cost is measured, not guessed. Price doesn't need the extra years because nothing compares against it any more.

What happens now
  • Task 7 is dropped; A12 is 6 tasks. Task 5's job loop passes start_date = 1990-01-01 for TRI and keeps 10 years for price.
  • Pre-1995 windows (none of A11's scenarios) have no TRI and show no benchmark.
  • Dot-com (Mar 2000) gets Nifty 50 and Nifty 500 rows; the 2003–07 run and 2008 also get those two; Midcap 150 and LargeMidcap 250 appear from Apr 2005.
  • The first morning after deploy fills everything; no runbook step.
  • Cost, speed and effect on other jobs (sizes and times measured 9 Oct; AWS rates derived from our own bill in Docs/2026-09-29-aws-staging-cost-analysis-and-reduction-plan.md):
    WhatTodayAfter
    Daily download4 price requests, ~1.9 MB, ~0.9 s+4 TRI requests: ~5.5 MB, ~2 s in total
    Rows in benchmark_index_history~9,900 (price, 10 years)~35,200 (+25,334 TRI rows, once)
    Rows written per day48
    Job run timeseconds of work inside a Fargate task (0.5 vCPU, 1 GB)about 1–2 s more per day; the first run inserts 25,334 rows once

    AWS cost: under $0.01 a month in total.

    • Fargate: the job's task costs about $0.0000071 per second (0.5 vCPU at ~$0.042/vCPU-hour + 1 GB at ~$0.0046/GB-hour, ap-south-1). A few extra seconds a day is about $0.001 a month; the first-run insert is a one-off fraction of a cent. The job already runs daily, so there's no new task, schedule or Terraform.
    • Data transfer: about 110 MB a month more, all inbound from the internet, which AWS doesn't charge for. It goes out through fck-nat, an EC2 instance with no per-GB fee (a managed NAT gateway would charge per GB; we don't use one).
    • RDS storage: about 25,000 small rows, a few MB with the index — a fraction of a cent a month.

    Loading speed for users: unaffected, and slightly better in one case.

    • The job runs in its own Fargate task at 06:00 IST, never inside the API container, so it can't slow a user's request.
    • Analytics screens read precomputed results. Benchmark lookups use the table's primary key, so going from ~10k to ~35k rows makes no practical difference.
    • Better: a household whose first transaction is before late 2016 no longer triggers an NSE download in the middle of its analytics recompute (benchmark.py:111), because the history is already stored.

    Other jobs: no interference.

    • benchmark-daily runs at 06:00 IST, alongside nav-daily (also 06:00); then scheme-master-daily 06:15, ter-daily 06:20 and the analytics recompute 06:30. Each job is its own Fargate task with its own CPU and memory.
    • The only shared pieces are RDS and fck-nat. A couple of seconds of extra download and 4 extra rows a day put no measurable load on either, and the job finishes long before the 06:30 recompute, which then reads fresh TRI.
    • The 9 PM–5 AM stop window isn't affected: RDS and fck-nat are back up from about 4:50 AM.
    • NSE sees 8 requests a day from us instead of 4, on the same host the job already uses daily.
Not chosen
  • A. One-off backfill_tri_history.py (the Task 7 I proposed earlier); the daily job stays at 10 years.
  • C. Widen both TRI and price to 1990.
