Portfolio P/E and P/B

Tells the client how expensive the stocks inside their funds are compared with the Nifty, using a harmonic mean over the equity part of the portfolio. It is the only attribute with its own daily data feed.

The formula

A harmonic mean over the equity part only

Portfolio P/E = 1 ÷ Σ( wᵢ ÷ P/Eᵢ ) Portfolio P/B = 1 ÷ Σ( wᵢ ÷ P/Bᵢ ) wᵢ = equity exposure of stock i ÷ total equity exposure (rescaled so equity = 100%; debt, cash, gold left out) Loss-making stocks (P/E null or negative) leave the P/E sum, the rest rescaled, footnote "x% of equity excluded". P/B has no such exclusion. Worked example (spec) portfolio 25.0x P/E and 3.35x P/B vs Nifty 50 22.8x and 3.10x → about 10% and 8% more expensive

Q28Your call

Which P/E, and are P/E and P/B comparable?

The issue

NSE's PE_<date>.csv has two columns, SYMBOL P/E and ADJUSTED P/E. The spec does not say which to use. Yahoo's trailingPE and priceToBook come from a different vendor and may use different earnings (standalone or consolidated) than NSE's. screener.in's own page is consolidated. The investigation notes the RELIANCE P/E from NSE's file and from Yahoo "differ, as expected".

Why it matters

The portfolio P/E mixes sources by stock and the P/B always comes from a different source than the P/E. The two numbers then answer slightly different questions, and the Nifty 50 comparison (index P/E from niftyindices.com) is a third methodology.

Options
  1. AP/E from NSE's SYMBOL P/E column. P/B from Yahoo. Show the source line under the chart. Accept that they differ.
  2. BUse Yahoo for both P/E and P/B so the pair is internally consistent; use NSE only as the fallback.
  3. CA, but compute P/B as price from NSE's own data divided by Yahoo's book value so at least the price is shared.
Recommended

A. NSE's data is the lower-risk source and the decision record already prefers it for everything it covers. I have not tested which column matches NSE's index P/E for the Nifty 50, which would settle it.

After your answer
  • I would run that one comparison from the Nifty 50 constituents before writing the aggregation, as a test fixture.
Q29Your call

Compare against Nifty 50 only, or Nifty 500 too?

The issue

The spec's UI compares the portfolio with Nifty 50. The sourcing covers both Nifty 50 and Nifty 500 index figures, and the fetch universe includes both lists.

Why it matters

Most Indian equity portfolios are broader than Nifty 50. Nifty 500 is the closer comparison for mid and small cap heavy holders, and fetching it costs nothing extra.

Options
  1. ANifty 50 only, as drawn.
  2. BNifty 50 and Nifty 500 as two rows on each bar.
  3. CNifty 500 only.
Recommended

B. Same endpoint and request shape, one more series.

After your answer
  • The universe query already includes the 500 list.
Q30Your call

How much of the equity must have data before we show P/E and P/B?

The issue

Each stock can fail on its own: delisted or merged, not on NSE (BSE-only), Yahoo has no P/B and screener.in has none either, or a loss-making stock excluded from P/E only. The spec lists the footnotes but sets no limit.

Why it matters

A portfolio P/E built from 40% of the equity is a number that looks authoritative and describes less than half the money.

Options
  1. AAlways show, with a footnote of the percentage excluded.
  2. BShow only if at least 70% of equity exposure has the ratio. Otherwise say "not enough data" and list what is missing.
  3. CShow only if 90% have it.
Recommended

B. A number shown with a footnote still gets read as the answer.

After your answer
  • The 70% line is a constant I picked, not a measured figure; you can change it.

no decision needed
The problem

Task 6 specifies the fetch client's behaviour in detail, down to the fallback chain and crumb reuse. It has no step for the stock_master model, its migration, a job script, or a Terraform entry, and does not say how long the daily run takes.

Evidence
Plan Task 6; infra/modules/scheduler/main.tf.

The daily feed is the only part of Sub-project 2 that runs every day for the whole year. With no job and no schedule, the table would stay empty, and attribute 15 would show footnotes for every stock.

The fix

  • Run-time planning figure: at about 0.3 s per request, 3,000 stocks take about 15 minutes. That is arithmetic on the planned delay, not a measured run.
  • One *RefreshResult dataclass, same shape as amfi_ter_client.py; never overwrite a good row with a failed fetch.
Effect on the current system
  • One new table, no foreign keys. No change to scheme_holdings (read only, for the ISIN universe).
  • New outbound traffic to NSE, Yahoo and screener.in from Fargate: see Q6.
Effect on the plan

F16Plan fix

The second cache-invalidation trigger collapses into job order

no decision needed
The problem

The plan and spec ask for two separate triggers for attribute 15's cache (a disclosure reaching WRITTEN, or a fresher stock_master.as_of_date) and four tests proving each works alone. In the code there is no event hook of that kind. Analytics results refresh on a CAS import or retry, or at the daily 06:30 run, which recomputes every household.

Why it matters

Building the two triggers means finding every household that holds the affected funds and starting a recompute for each. That is a new mechanism and a Fargate task per household, for data that changes at most once a day.

The fix
  • If Q3 is answered A or C: put both data jobs before 06:30 (F3). Results are then at most one day old with no trigger code, and the four invalidation tests become one test that the scheduler entries run before the recompute entry.
  • If Q3 is answered B (event-driven), build the triggers and keep the four tests as written.
Effect on the current system
  • No existing code changes under A or C. Under B, the disclosure job gains a step that selects households and calls try_claim_recompute and the dispatcher for each.
Effect on the plan
Task 6's last two tests are replaced or kept depending on Q3. The visual map and spec section 7.3 describe two triggers, so they get a dated note.
