Q3Your call

How do new disclosures reach a client's screen?

The issue

The plans say a disclosure reaching WRITTEN invalidates the affected households' cached results, and that attribute 15 has a second trigger on fresher stock data. No such event exists today. A household recompute starts after a CAS import or an on-demand retry, or from the daily run at 06:30 IST that recomputes every household. Nothing recomputes households when a table they read changes. The new disclosure job is scheduled for 07:00, after that run.

Why it matters

Without a choice, a new disclosure appears the day after next. Building event-driven recompute means finding every household that holds the affected funds and starting one Fargate task per household.

Options
  1. AUse the daily run. Schedule both new data jobs ahead of 06:30 so a new disclosure shows the next morning. No new trigger code.
  2. BAfter the disclosure job, recompute only the households that hold a fund that just got a new batch.
  3. CA now; B only if you find next-morning too slow.
Recommended

C. Holdings change monthly and the stock fundamentals change daily. Next-morning is enough for both.

After your answer
  • The four invalidation tests in the A15 plan become a schedule-order check.
  • Jobs move to about 05:45 (F3); Terraform edit only, you apply.
Q4Your call

How much of the look-through table is stored per scope?

The issue

Every household analytics result is stored as one JSON payload per scope and per section, and every Analytics load returns all sections for the scope in one request (and again on every poll while recomputing). There is no size limit anywhere. A full look-through table of a few hundred stocks, each with its contributing funds, is hundreds of kilobytes per scope. Five scopes for a 4-member household multiply it.

Why it matters

The Analytics page is the one most users open first.

Options
  1. AStore the full table for each scope.
  2. BStore the top 100 stocks per scope with a note that the full list is shorter than the portfolio. Overlap capped by Q25.
  3. CStore the top 25 and drop "View all" from this release.
Recommended

B. 100 rows cover the large majority of exposure for typical diversified portfolios. I have not measured this on real data; the first real batch would show it.

After your answer
  • The cut-off is one constant; the row count shown is stated under the table.
Q5Your call

Do the five new sections go into the PDF export?

The issue

The PDF is built in the browser from each loaded section and has no schema on the backend. Sub-project 1's investment_withdrawal section was never added to it, so the export already omits one live section. Sub-project 1's other plans listed the PDF print view in their change map.

Why it matters

Heavy charts (a heatmap, two donuts) can slow the page the export waits on; the export waits 15 seconds.

Options
  1. AAdd all five new sections to the export.
  2. BLeave the new sections out of the PDF for now.
  3. CAdd the five, and add investment_withdrawal too, since it is the same edit in the same files.
Recommended

C. Same files, same pattern, and one gap closed.

Q6Your call

Test every source from the AWS network before building?

The issue

Every source was verified from a development machine. The job runs in Fargate behind the fck-nat instance. NSE, Yahoo, AMC sites and the Internet Archive may block cloud addresses. This is the same unverified assumption already listed under Priority 1 for the 40 automatic factsheet AMCs.

Why it matters

A source that works on a laptop and fails in production would show up as a missing-AMC alert weeks after launch.

Options
  1. ABefore building, run one throwaway Fargate or bastion-side check that requests each source once (NSE files, Yahoo handshake, AMFI portal, Wayback, the 10 registered AMC hosts, the Nifty lists) and reports status codes. Covers Priority 1's question for the same AMC hosts as well.
  2. BFind out at the first staging run.
  3. CCheck only Yahoo, NSE and the Archive, the three most likely to block.
Recommended

A. It is cheap, it uses the guide pattern that starts and stops the bastion, and it answers Priority 1 for free.

After your answer
  • I write the check as a read-only script with a short guide; you start the bastion.
Q7Your call

What do we show when many of a client's funds have no stock data?

The issue

Some funds will have no batch: AMCs not yet onboarded, the 6 manual ones, the 5 that publish nothing, and funds that are late. The spec says a banner "N of your M funds haven't published this month's holdings yet". That wording is wrong for an AMC that never will, and it counts funds, not money.

Why it matters

"Top 10 stocks represent 38%" computed over 30% of the portfolio is misleading. "Yet" promises something that for some AMCs never arrives.

Options
  1. ABanner always, counted by value: "Based on 62% of your portfolio. 3 funds not covered." Two reasons shown separately: "not published yet" and "not available from this AMC".
  2. BA, and hide headline sentences (concentration, top-10 share, portfolio P/E) when covered value is under 50%.
  3. CShow nothing for the five attributes until at least 50% is covered.
Recommended

B. Honest at every level and never silent. The 50% line is my pick, not a measured number.

After your answer
  • One shared coverage figure computed in the shared table and read by all five sections.
