#1

Opening balance thrown away

In this passBug · biggest causeProven on real + synthetic filesDecided: hybrid cost + approximate date

In plain words

A CAS only lists transactions inside the period you ask for. Everything you owned before that is summed into one line, the opening balance. Unifolio never saves that line, so the shorter the lookback, the more of your money disappears. Anyone who invested before 2016 loses value even on a 10-year file.

What you'd see

The ₹20 Cr test investor sees ₹10.5 Cr from the 10-year file and ₹11 L from the FY file. Your own FY file shows ₹2.8 L instead of ₹41.9 L. A fund with no activity in the period vanishes completely.

Decided: the hybrid cost method

  1. Prefer the CAS's own cost. Every CAS prints "Total Cost Value" for what you hold now. Opening-lot cost = valuation.cost − FIFO cost of in-period purchases still held. That's exact whenever the fund had no in-period sales, which is the common case for long-held funds.
  2. Sanity check: the resulting cost per unit must sit between the fund's lowest and highest NAV before the start date (from nav_history). If it doesn't (in-period sales ate into the opening lot, or the CAS cost is missing), go to step 3.
  3. Fallback: NAV on the start date. Cost = opening units × NAV on S. Invested amount is then understated (it ignores pre-S growth), so the lot is marked cost_source = nav_on_start and the fund shows "cost approximate".
  4. One lot per fund, dated S, with origin = cas_opening. The earliest-statement rule (diagram below) guarantees only one exists per folio.

Can we show an approximate purchase date? Yes

We can't know the exact dates, but two things are certain or estimable:

  • Certain: the units were bought before the statement start date S. So the holding period is at least today − S. For a 10-year statement that alone proves long-term for tax.
  • Estimate: cost per unit = the average price paid. Find when the fund's NAV last crossed that price before S in nav_history: that date is a fair "around when" for the average purchase. If the NAV never reached it (CAS cost unusual), show only "before S".

What are the "Franklin units"? (explained)

Background. In April 2020 Franklin Templeton shut six of its debt funds, including Franklin India Low Duration Fund. They couldn't sell enough bonds to pay everyone redeeming during the COVID panic. Investors were locked in. As the underlying bonds matured or were sold over 2021–2022, SEBI and the courts had Franklin return the money in instalments ("tranches").

How that appears on a CAS. Each tranche is printed as Payment - Units Extinguished: some of your units are cancelled at that day's NAV and the money is paid to your bank, which is effectively a forced redemption. Occasionally a tranche was cancelled or corrected, and the CAS prints Payment - Units Extinguished-Reversed, giving the units back. KFintech prints those reversed rows in brackets, which normally means a negative number, even though the units come back.

The side pocket. In January 2020, before the wind-up, the fund held Vodafone Idea bonds that were downgraded. SEBI rules let a fund move such bad bonds into a separate segregated portfolio. Every investor received the same number of units in "Franklin India Low Duration Fund – Segregated Portfolio 1", worth about ₹0. It's a separate line on the CAS that only pays out if Vodafone Idea repays.

What Unifolio does with all this (tested with a synthetic statement): extinguishments are saved as redemptions, which is correct. casparser uses the running balance to flip the bracketed "-Reversed" row back to a positive purchase, also correct. The segregated units are saved as segregation but not counted, so the unit count is wrong while the value is still about ₹0. And because the fund is now fully paid out, its realised gain (₹26.4 L in the test) disappears (#9).

Decided: cost of gifted units (recommended option)

A gift's new lot is priced at the NAV on the gift date, which is what the CAS prints, so value, invested amount and XIRR work immediately. The row keeps type = gift_in, so a future tax report (#20) can ask the user for the donor's original cost and purchase date. Indian tax law uses the donor's cost for gifted units. Nothing is guessed silently.

Decided 5 Oct: gift cost = NAV on the gift date (CAS value), type kept as gift_in for a later donor-cost prompt.
